## Background: Von Neumann Poker

Von Neumann poker is a foundational model of strategic decision-making in competitive environments. It's a two-player zero-sum game designed to study bluffing, betting, and optimal play without real poker's combinatorial complexity.

The classic game: each player receives a hand strength uniformly drawn from [0, 1]. The bettor acts first, choosing to check or bet a fixed amount. The caller responds by calling or folding. Higher hand strength wins.

This elegant abstraction has been thoroughly analyzed. But what if we allow variable bet sizing?

- **Fixed-Bet Continuous Poker (FBCP)**: Bettor can only bet a predetermined amount.
- **No-Limit Continuous Poker (NLCP)**: Bettor can bet any positive amount.

Real poker sits between these extremes. Minimum and maximum bet constraints have a significant effect on strategy.

## Limit Continuous Poker (LCP)

We study the variant where the bettor can bet any amount $s \in [L, U]$. This unifies FBCP and NLCP:
- When $L = U = B$, we recover FBCP with fixed bet size $B$
- When $L \to 0$ and $U \to \infty$, we approach NLCP

Everything in between is a new strategic landscape.

## Main Results

### 1. Nash Equilibrium Strategy Profile

The unique admissible Nash equilibrium has an elegant structure:

**Bettor's strategy:** Partition hands into three regions:
- **Bluffing [0, x₂]**: Weak hands bluff, with larger bets on weaker hands
- **Checking [x₂, x₃]**: Medium hands check (too weak to value bet, too strong to bluff)
- **Value betting [x₃, 1]**: Strong hands bet, with larger bets on stronger hands

**Caller's strategy:** Calling threshold $c(s)$ increases with bet size—larger bets require stronger hands to call, reflecting pot odds.

### 2. Closed-Form Game Value

The bettor's expected payoff under optimal play:

$$V(r,t) = \frac{1 - r^3 - t^3}{14 - 2r^3 - 2t^3}$$

where $r = L/(1+L)$ and $t = 1/(1+U)$.

**Key discovery:** Perfect symmetry in game value—$V(L, U) = V(1/U, 1/L)$. Increasing the maximum bet constraint has *exactly the same effect* as decreasing the minimum bet constraint (in reciprocal form). This reveals a deep duality between the bettor's betting freedom and the caller's defensive incentives.

### 3. Counterintuitive Parameter Effects

Increasing the upper bet limit $U$ doesn't uniformly help the bettor.

When $U$ increases:
- **Strong hands** benefit from larger value bets ✓
- **Intermediate hands** suffer because the caller becomes more conservative across all bet sizes ✗
- Each value-betting hand strength has a critical threshold $U^*$ above which expected value decreases

This strategic interdependence is counterintuitive: more favorable game parameters can decrease payoffs in specific regions. It mirrors real poker phenomena where deeper stacks help premium hands but hurt slightly-above-average hands.

### 4. Convergence to Classical Variants

LCP smoothly interpolates between FBCP and NLCP:
- As $L \to B$ and $U \to B$, strategies and game value converge to FBCP
- As $L \to 0$ and $U \to \infty$, they converge to NLCP

This validates LCP as a genuine unified framework, not an arbitrary variant.

## Implications

The equilibrium structure illuminates fundamental principles of imperfect information games:

**Information revelation through bet sizing:** Balanced strategies must mix strong and weak hands at each bet size to prevent exploitation. Your bet sizes leak information—equilibrium play manages this leak strategically.

**Structure and Symmetry:** The elegant form of the game value as a function of $r$ and $t$ suggests that these values, rather than the obvious $L, U$ parametrization, are somehow more fundamental to the structure of the game. The symmetry $V(r, t) = V(t, r)$ especially suggests a deeper connection between these two quantities.

**Tractable complexity:** By studying a stripped-down model, we understand principles that generalize to far more complex games, without drowning in combinatorial detail.
